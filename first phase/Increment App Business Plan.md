# Increment App Business Plan

**App name:** Increment  
**Chinese descriptor:** 可理解输入练习  

## 1. App Overview

Increment is an AI-assisted English practice app for Chinese university learners at beginner to intermediate level. It addresses a familiar gap: learners can understand written English or subtitled videos, but struggle to understand audio independently and express the same ideas in their own words.

The name connects the programming expression `i++` with the English-learning principle of `i+1`: taking a manageable step beyond the learner's current ability. Inspired by online videos, Increment aims to make understandable input and repeated practice part of a sustainable learning routine.

The app connects listening, speaking, reading, and writing around short, meaningful materials matched to the learner's interests and approximate level:

- **Listening:** understand short audio with subtitle support that can be reduced gradually.
- **Speaking:** practise shadowing and retell the material in the learner's own words.
- **Reading:** explore short texts with difficulty adjustment and contextual hints.
- **Writing:** write a brief retelling, receive feedback, and revise it.

AI will support three user needs: adjusting text difficulty, explaining difficult language in context, and providing feedback on written retellings. Useful expressions and difficult material can be revisited in later sessions.

The intended value is a clear daily routine that helps learners move from supported understanding to independent use. Whether this routine improves engagement and learning will be evaluated through user trials.

## 2. Identify a Unique Selling Proposition

**Increment helps subtitle-dependent English learners move from understanding to independent expression through one connected practice routine, supported by AI difficulty adjustment, contextual hints, and retelling feedback.**

Its proposed differentiation combines three elements:

- **Manageable input:** materials are selected around interests and approximate ability, with AI text adjustment available when the language feels unsuitable.
- **Gradual independence:** learners reduce Chinese and English subtitle support as they become more familiar with the material.
- **Active use and review:** each material leads to a small speaking or writing task, followed by feedback and opportunities to practise again.

The learning unit is a meaningful text or situation. The four skills reinforce each other through the same material, giving users a practical answer to: “I understood it, but can I use it?”

This is the intended product positioning; user validation will determine whether learners find the combination distinctive and valuable.

## 3. Target a User Persona

### Primary persona: A subtitle-dependent university learner

The following persona is a working hypothesis to be checked through interviews.

| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| Age and location | 18–25, studying at a university in China |
| English background | Several years of formal English education; stronger at reading familiar material than listening or spontaneous speaking |
| Learning habits | Saves videos and learning resources, uses subtitles or translation frequently, and practises inconsistently |
| Main frustrations | “I know the words, but cannot understand the audio.” “I understand the sentence, but cannot say it.” “I do not know which material suits my level.” |
| Goal | Understand interesting English content with less subtitle support and communicate more confidently in everyday situations |
| Interests | Campus life, technology, games, travel, music, and practical conversations |
| Usage context | Short practice sessions between classes or in the evening |
| Motivation to try Increment | A manageable routine, relevant content, and clear guidance after an expression attempt |

For example, this learner watches an English video with Chinese subtitles and understands the story, but cannot follow the same clip without them. Increment would guide the learner through a short piece of suitable material and then invite a brief independent response.

## 4. Use the Right Marketing Channels

The initial audience will be university learners reachable through campus communities. Marketing will demonstrate the practice experience and invite users to try one session.

| Channel | Planned activity | Reason for choosing it |
|---|---|---|
| Class and dormitory groups | Share a brief introduction and invite classmates to try a sample session | Direct access to the initial target audience and an opportunity to gather early feedback |
| University English clubs and study groups | Run a small seven-day practice challenge | Reach learners already interested in improving English and observe repeated use |
| Short-video platforms | Demonstrate supported listening, subtitle removal, and a short retelling | Make the proposed benefit visible through a concrete example |
| Content communities | Share practical examples of material selection, shadowing, and retelling | Reach learners discussing the problems Increment aims to address |

The main message will be: **“Understand English with support. Practise until you can use it yourself.”**

The proposed acquisition path is: see a demonstration, try one free session, join a short practice challenge, and decide whether to continue. The campus pilot will come first; broader promotion will depend on feedback and repeat use.

## 5. Financial Key Metrics for Your Mobile App Business Plan (Optional)

The course prototype will focus on validating demand. A paid model has not been selected, and no revenue or cost figures are claimed.

If Increment is developed into a commercial product, the following metrics would support business decisions:

| Metric | Business question |
|---|---|
| Customer acquisition cost (CAC) | How much marketing expenditure is required to acquire a new user? |
| Operating cost per active user | What does it cost to serve an active learner, including AI usage, hosting, and content preparation? |
| Free-to-paid conversion rate | If a paid offering is introduced, what proportion of free users become paying customers? |
| Average revenue per paying user | How much revenue does each paying user generate in a defined period? |
| Monthly recurring revenue (MRR) | If subscriptions are introduced, how much recurring subscription revenue is generated each month? |
| Customer lifetime value (LTV) | What contribution is expected from a paying customer over their relationship with the app? |

Initial expenditure would include content preparation, AI service usage, hosting, and pilot promotion. Estimates will be based on actual usage and supplier prices when services are selected.

## 6. Channels to Validate an App (Optional)

Validation will test whether the target problem is significant, whether users understand the proposed value, and whether they choose to return.

| Channel | Planned validation | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|---|
| Campus learner interviews | Interview 8–12 learners about subtitles, material difficulty, and expression difficulties | Recurring problems, current alternatives, and reasons to try or reject the app |
| Class and study-group surveys | Test the product description and invitation to a trial | Interest, willingness to participate, and preferred practice topics |
| Prototype sessions | Ask at least 10 learners to try a representative practice session | Task completion, confusing steps, and perceived usefulness |
| Seven-day campus pilot | Invite at least 10 learners to use a usable prototype over one week | First-session completion, repeat use, drop-off reasons, and feedback on AI assistance |

The key questions are whether learners find the material manageable, value gradual subtitle removal, find retelling feedback helpful, and want to practise again. Early trials will guide changes to the product and marketing message. They will assess demand and usability rather than establish long-term learning effectiveness.
